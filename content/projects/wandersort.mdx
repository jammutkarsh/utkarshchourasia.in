Years of photos end up spread across phones, SD cards and old drives: three copies of the
same picture, RAW and JPG files separated from each other, Live Photos split into a
`.HEIC`, a `.MOV` and an `.AAE`, and edited versions sitting next to the originals with no
way to tell which is which. Finding one clip means digging through `DCIM`, `Backup` and
`New Folder (2)`.

WanderSort fingerprints every file, works out which ones are duplicates or belong
together, and plans a folder tree you can read, such as `2024/08_August/02_04/<city>/`.
You review and correct the plan before anything is copied, and `check` later proves
nothing in the library has rotted. Everything runs on your machine in one Go binary, so
there are no uploads, no subscriptions, and no AI deciding what is relevant.

It runs as a full-screen TUI, or step by step with `add`, `organise` and `copy`. The
last three also run unattended from cron, with documented exit codes.
